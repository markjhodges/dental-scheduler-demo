# Dental Scheduler — Draft Specification

## Product scope

- Build for one dental practice during initial development, with expansion to multiple practices as a later product goal.
- The initial practice is in California, United States. Design for applicable federal and California healthcare, privacy, security, accessibility, and consumer protection requirements from the start. Expansion to other states requires a new jurisdiction review.
- Let patients use a web app on desktop or phone. Native iOS and Android apps are a later goal.
- Design the product to integrate with Dentrix Ascend. Until API access is available, simulate patient records and scheduling actions using test data.
- Aim to let patients self book every appointment type, including routine visits and treatment plan work. Complex types will need practice specific rules before they can safely be offered.
- At initial release, confirm cleanings and exams immediately when a valid slot is available. Complex treatment booking requests should go to staff for review once the treatment booking feature is ready. Expand immediate confirmation as scheduling rules become reliable.
- The initial cleaning and exam release can launch while treatment cost estimation is still being validated. Resolve the treatment estimate method and patient cost display rules before releasing patient facing treatment booking.

## Patient accounts and identity

- Let a patient create an account linked to the correct Dentrix Ascend patient record.
- Candidate matching flow: ask for name and date of birth, then send a verification code to the phone number already on file. If multiple records have the same name and birth date, ask for the phone number on record to help distinguish them without exposing candidate records. If the result is still ambiguous, use staff verification.
- Offer verification by email on record when phone verification fails. If neither contact method works, direct the person to call the practice for manual verification. Keep the final identity proofing and account recovery process open for further design.
- A verified user may book for their own record and, when authorized, for linked minor children. Being the insurance subscriber or the subscriber's spouse may help staff identify a possible guardian but does not by itself prove legal authority. Verify parent or guardian authority and apply any state specific limits before granting access to a child's record. Source: [HHS on parental access](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/am-i-my-childs.pdf).
- Let an authorized guardian view the child's appointment history and treatment costs. Adults should book for themselves in the app; someone seeking to book for another adult must call the practice. Keep delegated adult access as a future question. Sharing a phone number alone must not grant access to another person's record.
- After sign in, show the patient's past appointments, upcoming appointments, favorite providers, treatment plans, and submitted feedback.
- Make Overview the patient's consolidated home: upcoming visits first, previous appointments with inline feedback below them, and treatment plans below the combined recare and imaging panel in the side column. Keep booking as a separate task view; remove separate patient History & feedback and Treatment tabs.

## Routine scheduling and recare

- Initially allow immediate booking for adult cleanings, child cleanings, new patient exams, and periodic exams when all required resources are available.
- Show available slots based on existing bookings, provider availability, appointment length, eligible provider and operatory combinations, and any other required resources. Enter eligible combinations manually at first; consider data based recommendations later.
- Give the practice admin a monthly calendar to set and adjust each provider's availability for the coming six months. Let the admin assign recurring workdays, mark individual days off or on, distinguish providers by color, and save the changes. Existing staff use repeating reminders on the Ascend schedule, so the relationship between those reminders, Ascend Provider Hours, and the app's availability calendar must be resolved before live booking.
- Base appointment length on visit type, patient needs, and provider. Initial practice examples: existing adult cleaning 60 minutes; child cleaning about 30 minutes; new patient cleaning 90 minutes; fillings about 60–120 minutes, sometimes shorter or longer by provider; crowns about 120 minutes when prepared, potentially much longer for extensive work. Confirm the duration of exam only visits and new child visits. These are starting values, not final booking rules.
- A cleaning usually involves both a hygienist and a dentist exam. Account for the dentist's participation, particularly when the patient requests a specific dentist for the exam.
- For new patients, explain and provide a way to submit the required intake files before the appointment. The exact file list and submission method need practice input.
- Use appointment history to identify familiar providers. Show the patient a preferred provider and let them filter available slots to that provider. Let patients explicitly favorite a provider.
- Show the patient's next recare due date when recorded in Dentrix Ascend. For simulated data, use a practice configured interval; six months plus one day after the prior visit is the proposed initial example.
- Treat the recare due date and insurance coverage eligibility as separate facts. Verify the patient's actual plan frequency and prior covered visits before saying a cleaning is covered. Plans can allow two visits per plan year or require a six month interval, so the proposed example cannot be treated as universal. Sources: [Ascend recare due dates](https://hsps.pro/DentrixAscend/Help/handling_patient_visits.htm), [ADA on frequency limits](https://www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ada-org/files/resources/practice/dental-insurance/responding-to-claim-rejections.pdf).
- Aim to show the patient's expected out of pocket cost for cleanings and exams. Display an estimated $0 only when a current benefits check supports it; otherwise show that the cost is still being checked. Label any displayed amount as an estimate because eligibility and benefits can change before the visit. The same transparency goal applies to treatments, subject to the treatment estimate launch requirement. Source: [ADA on changing benefits](https://www.ada.org/resources/practice/dental-insurance/pre-authorizations).
- Review the date of prior X-rays and tell patients when they may be due for imaging. Let them express whether they want X-rays as part of the visit and pass that preference to the practice. The treating dentist determines whether imaging is clinically needed after considering the patient's history and examination; there is no universal X-ray interval. Source: [ADA radiograph guidance](https://www.ada.org/resources/ada-library/oral-health-topics/x-rays-radiographs).
- Combine recare and imaging in a visual patient panel. Show the recorded insurance plan name and selectable columns/cards for cleaning, exam, and each tracked X-ray type, each with its next due date. Selecting a procedure reveals a horizontal timeline with a dot and the prior procedure date at the left, the due date at the right, and a filled line whose opacity/extent represents the elapsed percentage between those dates, clamped from 0% to 100%. At 100%, give the selected procedure a brighter glow and a clear text label that it is due. Handle missing dates without a fabricated progress value.
- In the synthetic demo, clearly label plan names and procedure dates as illustrative. A recare reminder is not proof of insurance eligibility, and imaging reminders do not determine clinical need. Replace demo milestones with verified source data and practice-approved rules before use with real patients.

## Appointment changes

- Let patients move or cancel eligible upcoming appointments through the app.
- Let patients move or cancel online until 24 hours before the appointment, including the exact 24 hour mark. With less than 24 hours remaining, direct them to call the practice.
- Show a selected slot as pending while the patient completes booking. Confirm the appointment only after the scheduling system accepts it; if the slot is taken first, explain that it is unavailable and offer other times. Whether Ascend supports an actual temporary reservation remains unverified.
- For significant treatment appointments, notify the provider when a patient moves or cancels. The exact notification and approval workflow remains to be defined with the practice.

## Treatment plan booking

- Show current treatment plans with predicted treatment costs, estimated insurance coverage, and estimated patient responsibility only when a reliable estimate can be obtained. Always state that predicted costs can change and estimates may be inaccurate.
- Resolve and validate the insurance estimate method before launching patient facing treatment booking. If a reliable estimate cannot be produced for a plan, do not display a cost; decide with the practice whether that plan can still be booked online without one.
- Let patients book treatment plans online, with the goal that every plan can eventually be self booked.
- Assign treatment work to the dentist who diagnosed or prescribed it. Use the dentist associated with the plan's exam where possible; the most recent exam may help identify the dentist when that association is missing.
- If the intended dentist is temporarily unavailable, send the booking request for admin review. If the dentist has left the practice, route affected bookings and new requests for admin review. The admin can reassign or cancel an appointment and explain the decision to the patient.
- Support one visit and multiple visit treatments, including appointments that may need to be hours apart. Define procedure order, gaps, room needs, and other constraints with the dentist before automating these cases.
- Let dentists set a treatment's expected duration and scheduling method. Offer a duration recommendation based on comparable past appointments; the dentist makes the final choice.

## Feedback and practice insights

- After an appointment, let the patient submit a one to five star rating with optional comments or concerns.
- Let patients review their own submitted ratings and comments.
- On Overview, list previous appointments below upcoming visits. Each row shows the visit date, provider names, procedures recorded for that appointment, and the patient's submitted ratings. A Share feedback control opens an inline form with a provider dropdown, stars, and optional comment. Hide the control once feedback has been submitted for every rateable provider on that completed appointment; do not offer it for incomplete or cancelled appointments. If procedure detail is unavailable, say so rather than treating the appointment type as a verified procedure list.
- Let only the practice admin or owner view all patient ratings and comments. Show each provider's overall rating, with comments linked to the relevant appointment in the admin view.

## US compliance and security requirements

- Treat identifiable patient, appointment, treatment, insurance, feedback, and intake information as protected health information when handled for the practice. Use synthetic data for development and demos until the practice and software operators are ready to handle real patient data.
- Define permitted uses and disclosures of patient information with the practice. Support the practice's privacy notice and a clear route for patients to exercise access and correction rights; document whether requests are handled in this app or by practice staff. Source: [HHS Privacy Rule overview](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/laws-regulations/index.html).
- Design account, staff, dentist, and admin roles so each user can access only the patient information and actions appropriate to their role. Include unique user accounts, authentication, access review, and audit records for access and changes. Verify a person's authority before allowing access to another patient's information. Sources: [HHS Security Rule](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/laws-regulations/index.html), [HHS representative verification](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/how-would-a-covered-entity-know-if-someone-were-a-personal-representative/index.html).
- Protect patient data in transit and at rest, maintain backups and recovery procedures, and document a security risk analysis and risk management plan before using real patient data. These are design choices and planning tasks; the final safeguards must be assessed against the HIPAA Security Rule and the practice's actual risks. Source: [HHS Security Rule](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/laws-regulations/index.html).
- Determine which party operates the app for the practice and which hosting, messaging, analytics, support, and other vendors receive patient data. Put required business associate agreements and data use terms in place before sharing protected health information with those parties. Sources: [HHS on app developers and business associates](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/business-associates/index.html), [HHS cloud guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/health-information-technology/cloud-computing/index.html).
- Plan for suspected security incidents and breaches: detection, investigation, documentation, practice notification, and applicable patient and regulator notifications. Source: [HHS Breach Notification Rule](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/breach-notification/index.html).
- Review state specific rules for minor and guardian access, consent, record retention, breach response, and patient communications before live use. When expanding to other practices, repeat this review for each state and practice arrangement. HHS notes that a parent's access to a child's records can depend on state law. Source: [HHS on parental access](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/am-i-my-childs.pdf).
- Build the patient website and app to be accessible to people with disabilities, and verify the applicable ADA requirements for this practice's public facing services. Source: [US Department of Justice web accessibility guidance](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-guidance/).

### California requirements to design for

- Review the California Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (CMIA) alongside HIPAA when deciding what medical information the practice, app operator, and vendors may collect, display, or disclose. California's Attorney General identifies CMIA as a protection for medical information and notes that it can apply to health apps. Sources: [California DOJ healthcare privacy guidance](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/media/healthcare-guidance.pdf), [California DOJ on health apps](https://www.oag.ca.gov/privacy/privacy-enforcement-actions).
- Treat a minor's account and guardian access as a per-patient authorization decision. Some services or records may be confidential from a parent or guardian under California law; insurance subscriber status alone is not enough to grant access. Source: [California Department of Health Care Services guidance](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/calaim-transforming-medi-cal/authorization-to-share-confidential-member-information/).
- Provide a route for patients or authorized representatives to request copies of their dental records and track the request through fulfillment by the practice. The Dental Board of California states that dental offices must provide or transmit requested records within 15 days of receipt, subject to the applicable request requirements. Source: [Dental Board of California, May 2025](https://www.dbc.ca.gov/formspubs/newsletter_2025_05.pdf).
- Include California's breach notification rules in the incident response plan, in addition to HIPAA. Source: [California Attorney General breach reporting guidance](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/databreach/reporting).
- For an X-ray refusal, record the patient's preference and route any clinical decision to the dentist. The Dental Board says a patient may refuse X-rays, while a dentist may decline treatment if the images are necessary for diagnosis or treatment planning. Source: [Dental Board of California consumer FAQ](https://www.dbc.ca.gov/consumers/consumer_faqs).
- Before launch, have California healthcare privacy counsel review the app's data flows, family access, patient notices, vendor agreements, record handling, messaging, and whether California consumer privacy rules also apply to data outside medical records. Revisit the review as the product and states served change.

## Dentrix Ascend integration findings

- **Published capabilities:** Official Ascend Public API documentation describes appointment retrieval and write operations, including start time, duration, provider, operatory, procedures, and treatment plan visits. It also documents treatment cases, patient procedures, and patient recare endpoints. This supports the general integration approach, subject to granted access and testing. Sources: [Appointments V1](https://papidocs.hs1api.com/publicapi/endpoints/appointments-v1), [Treatment cases V1](https://papidocs.hs1api.com/publicapi/endpoints/txcases-v1), [Patient procedures V1](https://papidocs.hs1api.com/publicapi/endpoints/patientprocedures-v1), [production access requirements](https://papidocs.hs1api.com/publicapi/obtaining-prod-access).
- **Access path:** Henry Schein One directs developers to apply to its API Exchange and complete a security review and integration testing. Its production access guide calls for sandbox testing, documentation of the intended endpoints and error handling, and API team approval before production credentials are provided. Sources: [vendor onboarding](https://www.henryscheinone.com/dental-solutions/api-exchange/api-exchange-vendors/), [production access requirements](https://papidocs.hs1api.com/publicapi/obtaining-prod-access).
- **Availability API:** Ascend documents a Schedule Openings endpoint that calculates open slots for provider and operatory pairs, with filters for location, dates, provider, operatory, minimum length, booking type, and reason. Provider Hours must be configured in Ascend or the endpoint returns no openings. The documentation inspected does not describe a temporary slot hold; confirm reservation and conflict behavior in the sandbox. Source: [Schedule Openings V1](https://papidocs.hs1api.com/publicapi/endpoints/scheduleopenings-v1).
- **Commercial path:** Henry Schein One says practices developing their own applications may have to pay for API access, and its vendor enablement guide describes organization controlled activation of an integration. The exact fees, approval path for a one practice pilot, and terms for expansion to many practices require direct confirmation. Sources: [customer FAQ](https://www.henryscheinone.com/dental-solutions/api-exchange/customer-faqs/), [vendor enablement](https://papidocs.hs1api.com/publicapi/vendor-enablement-guide).
- **Estimates and availability:** Published endpoints do not by themselves establish that the app can reproduce Dentrix Ascend's patient facing treatment cost and insurance estimate for every unscheduled plan. Confirm the needed fields and calculation with the API team and practice. Likewise, validate the full open slot calculation against provider schedules, time off, operatories, and competing bookings before enabling live booking. Sources: [insurance claim estimate data](https://papidocs.hs1api.com/publicapi/endpoints/insuranceclaims-v1), [Appointments V1](https://papidocs.hs1api.com/publicapi/endpoints/appointments-v1).
- **Integration behavior to design:** Keep Ascend as the source for patient and appointment records once connected. Check current availability when a patient confirms a booking or change, and show a clear failure and retry path if the integration is unavailable. These are proposed product rules to confirm during implementation, not claims about the API.
- **Product distinction:** The published read and write lists for installed Dentrix are separate from Dentrix Ascend's cloud API; do not use installed Dentrix capabilities or pricing to infer Ascend capabilities or terms. Sources: [Dentrix API FAQ](https://ddp.dentrix.com/pages/faq), [Ascend Public API](https://papidocs.hs1api.com/publicapi/home).
- **Account access research:** HHS guidance says a practice should verify the identity and authority of someone seeking access to another patient's health information. A shared family phone number verifies control of that number, not authority over every linked patient. NIST treats SMS verification as a restricted authenticator and recommends an alternative for people who cannot use it. Sources: [HHS personal representative verification](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/how-would-a-covered-entity-know-if-someone-were-a-personal-representative/index.html), [NIST authentication guidance](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html).

## Open decisions

- **California review:** Obtain a California specific legal and privacy review before live patient data or public launch, including CMIA, minor access, patient record requests, breach response, and any consumer privacy rules that apply to the app's nonmedical data.
- **Data handling:** Who will legally operate and host the app, which vendors will process protected health information, and what business associate agreements are needed?
- **Identity:** What evidence will the practice use to verify that an adult has parent or guardian authority for a minor, especially when insurance subscriber and legal guardian differ? How will duplicate records and changed contact details be handled safely?
- **Due date:** Can the Ascend API provide each patient's recare due date and the plan details needed to check cleaning coverage? What should the app show when those details are missing?
- **New patient intake:** Which files and forms are required, when must they be submitted, and what happens if they are incomplete before the visit?
- **X-rays:** Which prior imaging data can be read from Ascend, what initial reminder rule should the practice use, and how should a patient's preference affect appointment length and staff review?
- **Availability:** Can the app's provider calendar write to Ascend Provider Hours, or should it only narrow Ascend's openings? How should hygienist and dentist availability be checked together?
- **Booking safety:** Can a slot be reserved temporarily through Ascend, or should the app treat the selection as tentative until appointment creation succeeds? Beyond cleanings and exams, which types can move to immediate confirmation first?
- **API access:** What are the fees, approval steps, sandbox access, and contract terms for a one practice pilot that may later expand to multiple practices?
- **Cancellation:** Which treatment appointments require provider notification or approval when changed?
- **Cost display:** Which Ascend fields or insurance sources can produce a reliable estimate for an unscheduled treatment plan? If no estimate is available, may the patient still book without seeing a cost?
- **Complex visits:** What appointment sequences, minimum gaps, provider and operatory requirements, and override options does the dentist need?
